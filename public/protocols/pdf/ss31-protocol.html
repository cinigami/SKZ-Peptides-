🔬 How SS-31 Works

SS-31 (Elamipretide) is a mitochondrial-targeted tetrapeptide (D-Arg-Dmt-Lys-Phe-NH₂) that selectively concentrates in the inner mitochondrial membrane. It works through the following mechanisms:

  • Cardiolipin Binding: Targets and stabilizes cardiolipin in the inner mitochondrial membrane — a critical phospholipid for mitochondrial structure and function
  • Electron Transport Chain: Stabilizes the electron transport chain complexes, improving efficiency of oxidative phosphorylation
  • ROS Reduction: Reduces reactive oxygen species (ROS) production at the source, protecting cells from oxidative damage
  • ATP Enhancement: Enhances ATP production by optimizing mitochondrial energy output
  • Membrane Integrity: Prevents cardiolipin peroxidation, maintaining mitochondrial membrane potential
  • Cell-Permeable: Rapidly crosses cell membranes and concentrates 1000-5000x in mitochondria within minutes

🔬 Reconstitution Protocol

Materials Needed:

  • 1x SS-31 10mg vial
  • Bacteriostatic Water (BAC Water)
  • Insulin syringes (29G-31G)
  • Alcohol swabs
  • Sterile work environment

Step-by-Step Reconstitution:

  1. Preparation
    • Wash hands thoroughly
    • Clean work surface with alcohol
    • Allow vials to reach room temperature (10-15 minutes)
    • Remove protective caps from both vials
  2. Sterilize
    • Wipe rubber stoppers of both vials with fresh alcohol swabs
    • Allow to air dry for 30-60 seconds
    • Do NOT blow on or wipe dry
  3. Reconstitution
    • Draw 1mL BAC Water into a sterile syringe
    • Insert needle into SS-31 vial at an angle
    • Inject BAC Water slowly down the inside wall of the vial
    • Do NOT spray directly onto powder
    • Gently swirl or roll between palms — do NOT shake
    • Allow to dissolve completely (2-3 minutes) until solution is clear
  4. Label & Store
    • Label vial with: SS-31, concentration, date reconstituted
    • Store immediately at 2-8°C (refrigerator)
    • Protect from light

🔬 Research Background

SS-31 (Elamipretide), also known as MTP-131 and Bendavia, is a cell-permeable mitochondrial-targeted tetrapeptide. It selectively targets cardiolipin, a unique phospholipid found almost exclusively in the inner mitochondrial membrane.

Key research milestones:

  • FDA Approved: Granted approval for the treatment of Barth syndrome (a rare mitochondrial disease)
  • Geographic Atrophy: Phase 2/3 clinical trials for dry age-related macular degeneration (geographic atrophy)
  • Mitochondrial Myopathy: Studied in primary mitochondrial myopathy patients with improvements in 6-minute walk test
  • Heart Failure: Investigated for heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)
  • Mechanism: Concentrates in mitochondria 1000-5000x within minutes of administration
  • Safety: Demonstrated favorable safety profile across multiple clinical trials
