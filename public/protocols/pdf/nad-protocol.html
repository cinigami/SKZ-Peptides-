🔬 How NAD+ Works

NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) is one of the most critical coenzymes in the body. Present in every living cell, NAD+ levels naturally decline with age — by age 50, levels can be 50% lower than at age 20. Here's how it works:

  • ATP Production: Essential coenzyme for cellular energy production — without NAD+, mitochondria cannot produce ATP efficiently
  • Sirtuin Activation: Activates sirtuins (SIRT1-7), the "longevity genes" that regulate aging, inflammation, and stress resistance
  • DNA Repair: Enables DNA repair via PARPs (Poly ADP-Ribose Polymerases) — critical for preventing mutations and maintaining genomic stability
  • Immune Regulation: Regulates immune response through CD38, a key enzyme in immune cell signaling
  • Circadian Rhythm: Supports healthy circadian clock function and sleep-wake cycles
  • Neuroprotection: Supports neuronal health and cognitive function through multiple pathways

🔬 Reconstitution Protocol

Materials Needed:

  • 1x NAD+ 500mg vial
  • Bacteriostatic Water (BAC Water)
  • Insulin syringes (29G-31G)
  • Alcohol swabs
  • Sterile work environment

Step-by-Step Reconstitution:

  1. Preparation
    • Wash hands thoroughly
    • Clean work surface with alcohol
    • Allow vials to reach room temperature (10-15 minutes)
    • Remove protective caps from both vials
  2. Sterilize
    • Wipe rubber stoppers of both vials with fresh alcohol swabs
    • Allow to air dry for 30-60 seconds
    • Do NOT blow on or wipe dry
  3. Reconstitution
    • Draw 2mL BAC Water into a sterile syringe
    • Insert needle into NAD+ vial at an angle
    • Inject BAC Water slowly down the inside wall of the vial
    • Do NOT spray directly onto powder
    • Gently swirl or roll between palms — do NOT shake
    • Allow to dissolve completely (3-5 minutes) until solution is clear
    • Note: NAD+ may take slightly longer to dissolve than peptides — be patient
  4. Label & Store
    • Label vial with: NAD+, concentration, date reconstituted
    • Store immediately at 2-8°C (refrigerator)
    • Protect from light

⏰ Administration Schedule

Timing:

  • Best time: Morning, after eating a meal or snack
  • ⚠️ Important: Do NOT inject on an empty stomach — significantly increases nausea and flushing
  • Frequency: Loading 2-3x/week → Maintenance 1-2x/week
  • Duration: Ongoing maintenance or 8-12 week intensive cycles

Injection Sites (Subcutaneous):

  • Preferred: Lower abdomen (2 inches from navel)
  • Alternative: Upper thigh, back of arm
  • Needle: 29G-31G insulin syringe
  • Depth: 4-6mm subcutaneous
  • Inject SLOWLY — fast injection increases flushing and discomfort
  • Rotate injection sites each session to prevent tissue irritation

Injection Steps:

  1. Eat a meal or substantial snack 30-60 minutes before injecting
  2. Hydrate well — drink at least 500mL of water
  3. Clean injection site with alcohol swab, let dry
  4. Draw correct dose from reconstituted vial
  5. Pinch skin at injection site
  6. Insert needle at 45-90° angle
  7. Inject VERY slowly (30-60 seconds for full dose) — this minimizes flushing
  8. Hold for 5-10 seconds before withdrawing
  9. Do not massage the site

⚠️ Important Safety Considerations

Potential Research Observations:

  • Flushing/warmth — This is NORMAL and expected. Feels like a warm wave through the body. Typically lasts 5-30 minutes. Slow injection reduces this significantly.
  • Nausea — Much worse on empty stomach. Always eat before injecting.
  • Headache (usually first few sessions only)
  • Fatigue during first few sessions (paradoxical — resolves as NAD+ levels normalize)
  • Injection site reactions (redness, mild swelling)
  • Sleep disturbance (avoid late-day injections)
  • Heart palpitations (rare — reduce dose if persistent)

🚫 Who Should Avoid:

✅ Tips to Minimize Side Effects:

  • Eat before injecting — #1 rule. A light meal 30-60 minutes prior dramatically reduces nausea

🔬 Research Background

NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) was first discovered in 1906 and has since been recognized as one of the most important molecules in biology. It's involved in over 500 enzymatic reactions and is essential for life.

Key research highlights:

  • Aging: NAD+ levels decline 50%+ between ages 40-60. Restoring NAD+ reverses many age-related cellular changes in animal models
  • Sirtuins: NAD+ is the required fuel for all 7 sirtuin enzymes — master regulators of aging and longevity
  • DNA Repair: PARP enzymes consume NAD+ to repair DNA damage. Low NAD+ = impaired repair capacity
  • Neurodegeneration: Research shows NAD+ depletion contributes to neuronal dysfunction in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's models
  • Metabolic Health: NAD+ supplementation improves insulin sensitivity and metabolic function in multiple studies
  • David Sinclair's Research: Harvard geneticist's work showing NAD+ precursors can reverse aging markers has driven global interest
